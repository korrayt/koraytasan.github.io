CREATIVE PROCESS

Building Musical Worlds

How image, place and sonic character can create a recognisable artist identity.

Music has a location even when it has no map

Some songs immediately bring a specific place to mind: a street after the rain, the light inside a home or a river at night. Thinking about a world rather than only a genre can help an artist create a distinctive and coherent body of work.

Start with a few recurring details

Choose a handful of recurring ideas: a colour palette, a certain kind of space, a production texture, an emotional question. They can return in different combinations without making each release feel identical.

Let every project speak differently

A cinematic soundtrack, an atmospheric electronic identity and a playful Ankara wedding tune do not need to sound alike. They do need an editorial point of view that makes it clear why each belongs under the same creative label.

Build an invitation, not a maze

The listener should be able to enter each world in one click. A short introduction, an understandable release list and a clear listening destination often do more than an elaborate page of decorative elements.
